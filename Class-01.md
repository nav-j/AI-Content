# **2. What is Artificial Intelligence?**

## **Definition**

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a technology that enables computer systems to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as learning, problem-solving, understanding language, recognizing images, making predictions and generating content.

**Simple definition for students:**

Artificial Intelligence is the ability of a computer or machine to perform tasks in an intelligent way by using data, patterns, algorithms and learned knowledge.

### **Real-world example**

Imagine a human being looking at a photograph of a cat.

A human can recognize it as a cat because the brain has learned what cats look like.

An AI system can also learn to identify cats after being trained on many images containing cats and other objects.

# **3. Why was Artificial Intelligence developed?**

Traditional computers are very good at following instructions, performing calculations and processing data. However, many real-world tasks are difficult to solve using only fixed instructions.

For example:

- Understanding human language.
- Recognizing a face.
- Detecting spam emails.
- Predicting weather.
- Recommending movies.
- Identifying diseases in medical images.
- Answering questions.
- Generating images and videos.

AI was developed to help computers handle complex tasks involving patterns, predictions, perception, language and decision-making.

# **4. How does AI work?**

AI does not magically think like a human. Most AI systems work using combinations of data, algorithms, mathematical calculations, models and computing power.

## **Basic AI working process**

**Step 1 — Data**

Collect examples, text, images, numbers or other information.

**Step 2 — Learning / Training**

The system finds patterns and relationships in the training data.

**Step 3 — AI Model**

The trained model captures patterns that can be used for tasks.

**Step 4 — Input**

A user provides a question, image, data or instruction.

**Step 5 — Output**

The AI model produces a prediction, response, classification or generated content.

### **Example: AI spam email detector**

Imagine an email system containing 10,000 emails.

- 5,000 spam emails
- 5,000 normal emails

The AI system examines patterns such as words, links, sender details and other signals. During training, it learns patterns associated with spam and normal emails.

When a new email arrives, it analyzes the email and predicts:

- Spam
- Not spam

## **A. Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI)**

Also called Weak AI.

AI designed to perform a specific task or a limited range of tasks.

Examples:

- Voice assistants
- Face recognition
- AI chatbots
- Recommendation systems
- Spam filters
- Translation tools

Today's AI applications are predominantly narrow AI systems, even when they can perform many tasks.

## **B. Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)**

A hypothetical form of AI that could learn, understand and perform a very broad range of intellectual tasks at human-like generality, including adapting to new tasks.

AGI remains a research goal, not an established capability of today's AI.

## **C. Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI)**

A hypothetical AI that would surpass human abilities across virtually all intellectual domains, including scientific reasoning, creativity and problem-solving.

ASI is a theoretical concept and does not currently exist.

| Type | Status       | Example                                         |
| ---- | ------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| ANI  | Exists today | Chatbots, image recognition                     |
| AGI  | Not achieved | Human-level general intelligence across domains |
| ASI  | Hypothetical | Intelligence beyond humans across domains       |

# **8. Limitations of AI**

AI is powerful, but it is not perfect.

[Why Richtech Robotics Stock Popped Today | The Motley Fool](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/9RX4QAhVNZiSTXq2uUrYjwGFzo_h0tlKdgIAUQgihZ-aNoLN5oJGmGJnTGmtig9Ycz3ZbFhfknw-U-2rDEsTQvkY2DotWVcnJJRuA3cNN2npVVOfT27RlAtcgYwoG_RqcwUYTuTTjTH5hAnOatK6Lcxko7b7IR8nOI46mhiV_6U?purpose=inline)

### **AI can make mistakes**

AI may generate incorrect answers, even when it sounds confident.

### **AI may have bias**

If its training data contains bias, its outputs may reflect those patterns.

### **AI may lack context**

It may misunderstand a user's question, intent or situation.

### **AI does not automatically know the truth**

An AI-generated answer is not necessarily a verified fact.

### **AI has privacy risks**

Students should not upload passwords, private financial information, confidential documents or sensitive personal data to AI tools without appropriate authorization and safeguards.

### **AI requires human responsibility**

Important decisions must be checked and made responsibly.

**Golden Rule of AI**

# **AI can assist you, but you must verify it.**

Never blindly trust an AI-generated answer.

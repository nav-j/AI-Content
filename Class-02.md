Artificial Narrow Intelligence is an AI system designed to perform a specific task or a limited set of tasks.

It can be highly efficient in its area of specialization but does not have general human-like intelligence across all domains.

### **Examples**

- Face recognition
- Voice assistants
- Spam detection
- Chatbots
- Google Translate
- Movie recommendations
- AI image generators
- Traffic prediction

### **Real-life example**

A face recognition system may identify a person in an image very accurately.

However, that does not mean it can automatically teach mathematics, repair a car, cook food, or understand every situation like a human being.

It has specialized capabilities.

**Key point:** Almost all AI tools students use today, including advanced chatbots, are considered forms of Narrow AI, despite their broad range of abilities.

Artificial General Intelligence refers to a hypothetical AI capable of understanding, learning and performing a wide range of intellectual tasks with generality comparable to human intelligence.

A hypothetical AGI could potentially:

- Learn new subjects.
- Adapt to unfamiliar tasks.
- Solve different kinds of problems.
- Transfer knowledge between fields.
- Understand new situations without needing a separate system for every task.

### **Example scenario**

Imagine one AI system that could:

- Learn Python programming.
- Teach mathematics.
- Plan a business.
- Learn to play a new game.
- Adapt to unfamiliar problems.
- Use its knowledge from one subject to solve problems in another.

This broad, flexible capability is part of the idea behind AGI.

AGI is not a clearly established, achieved capability. Researchers debate its definition and how to measure it.

Artificial Super Intelligence is a hypothetical form of AI that would exceed human intellectual capabilities across virtually all domains.

It could theoretically outperform humans in:

- Scientific research
- Strategic planning
- Problem-solving
- Creativity
- Decision-making
- Complex analysis

ASI is a theoretical concept. It does not currently exist.

### **Comparison table**

| Feature       | ANI                  | AGI                                 | ASI                                |
| ------------- | -------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Intelligence  | Task-specific        | General                             | Beyond human                       |
| Exists today? | Yes                  | Not established                     | No                                 |
| Scope         | Limited domain/tasks | Broad tasks                         | Virtually all intellectual domains |
| Example       | Spam detector        | Hypothetical human-level general AI | Hypothetical superintelligence     |

### **Classroom discussion**

Ask students:

> If an AI can write code, create images, answer questions, translate languages and analyze data, does that automatically mean it is AGI?

**Answer:** No. Performing many different tasks does not, by itself, establish human-level general intelligence, general understanding or the ability to reliably adapt to every new situation.

Rule-Based AI works using predefined rules created by developers or experts.

The system follows conditions and instructions to produce an output.

### **How it works**

For example:

```
IF temperature > 38:    PRINT "High fever"ELSE:    PRINT "Temperature normal"
```

The system checks a condition and produces a result based on a predefined rule.

### **Examples**

- Simple expert systems
- Rule-based eligibility checking
- Basic troubleshooting systems
- Fixed chatbot responses
- Rule-based fraud alerts

### **Real-life example: Traffic light**

[Скрытый смысл трех огней: почему светофор получил именно эти цвета — решение оказалось гениальным - новости за июнь 2026 - 24СМИ](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/8Xy7E6hvIH3LVQ73VOXB3pSfl2_R-VAiiGxbfTd0UMsqPIOGvaG9uiXD-L7JY562-_tLoEgKS1O3hKJVcbc3wO6FGaYpc2TJ-gPmJVr2JWY_UJoS48m04sAkCcW3dc0WFa38JmaAZaQjl-h9CvSxYrANS1rld9TmhITBuxLZP9A?purpose=inline)

A simple traffic light system may follow fixed rules:

- Red → Stop
- Yellow → Prepare to stop
- Green → Go

This is automation using rules, not necessarily AI.

### **Advantages**

- Easy to understand.
- Predictable.
- Easy to control.
- Works well when rules are clearly defined.

### **Disadvantages**

- Cannot automatically learn new patterns.
- Requires rules to be created and maintained.
- May struggle with complex or unexpected situations.

Predictive AI uses data and patterns to estimate or predict outcomes.

It often uses Machine Learning models trained on historical data.

### **Examples**

- Predicting house prices.
- Predicting weather.
- Predicting customer churn.
- Detecting fraud.
- Predicting sales.
- Estimating delivery time.
- Predicting disease risk from data.

### **How Predictive AI works**

**Historical Data**

Past sales, prices, records, customer behaviour

**Machine Learning Model**

Finds relationships and patterns

**Prediction**

Estimates a likely future result or class

### **Example: Predicting house prices**

Suppose a dataset contains:

| Area (sq ft) | Bedrooms | Location | Price    |
| ------------ | -------- | -------- | -------- |
| 1,000        | 2        | City A   | ₹25 lakh |
| 1,500        | 3        | City A   | ₹38 lakh |
| 2,000        | 3        | City A   | ₹50 lakh |
| 2,500        | 4        | City A   | ₹65 lakh |

A Machine Learning model can learn patterns between area, bedrooms, location and price.

When we give it details of a new house, it may predict its estimated price.

The prediction is an estimate, not a guarantee.

# **14. Teacher Notes & Explanation Guide**

## **Concepts that must be clear before ending the class**

| Concept           | Teacher should ensure students understand                         |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ANI               | AI focused on specific tasks; most deployed AI fits this category |
| AGI               | Hypothetical broadly capable general intelligence                 |
| ASI               | Hypothetical intelligence beyond humans across broad domains      |
| Rule-Based AI     | Uses explicit rules and conditions                                |
| Predictive AI     | Estimates outcomes using data and models                          |
| Generative AI     | Generates new content                                             |
| Conversational AI | Communicates with users through conversation                      |
| Computer Vision   | Understands/analyzes images and videos                            |
| Speech AI         | Processes speech and audio                                        |

### **Common student misunderstandings**

**Misunderstanding 1:** Every robot is AI.

Correction: A robot may operate using fixed instructions without AI.

**Misunderstanding 2:** ChatGPT is AGI because it can do many things.

Correction: Broad capability does not automatically prove general intelligence at human level.

**Misunderstanding 3:** Predictive AI always knows the future.

Correction: It estimates outcomes based on data and assumptions. Predictions can be wrong.

**Misunderstanding 4:** Generative AI copies and pastes content.

Correction: Generative models produce outputs based on learned statistical patterns. Outputs can resemble training examples, so originality, accuracy and copyright still require consideration.

**Misunderstanding 5:** All AI systems learn by themselves after deployment.

Correction: Many models do not automatically update their knowledge or learn from each interaction. Updates depend on the system's design.

- Computer Vision — image/video understanding
- Speech AI — voice and audio processing

- Predictive AI → estimates what may happen.
- Generative AI → creates something new.
- Computer Vision → analyzes visual information.
- Speech AI → works with spoken/audio information.
